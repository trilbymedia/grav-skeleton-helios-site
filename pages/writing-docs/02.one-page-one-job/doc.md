Once you know the question a page answers, the next step is to make sure the page answers only that question. Most pages that feel hard to read are really two or three pages sharing one address.

===

## The four kinds of page

Documentation pages fall into four kinds, and each one has a different job:

- **A tutorial** takes a newcomer from nothing to something working, one step at a time. It makes choices for the reader so they don't have to.
- **A how-to guide** gets a reader who already knows the basics through one task, like moving a site to a new server.
- **A reference page** lists facts: every option, every command, every field. It is complete and dry on purpose.
- **An explanation** says why things are the way they are, and gives the reader a model of how the parts fit together.

Look at your page. If it starts as a tutorial, stops halfway to list every option, then ends with a paragraph on the design, it is doing three jobs.

## Splitting a mixed page

Split by job, not by length. Keep the address the page has now for the job most readers come for, and give the other parts pages of their own:

1. Copy the page twice, once for each part that is moving out.
2. Cut each copy down to its one job.
3. Link the pages to each other where a reader would naturally move between them.
4. Leave the original address on the main job, so links from elsewhere still work.

> [!NOTE]
> A tutorial should link to the reference page for an option, not repeat the reference table. A reader who wants every option will follow the link, and a reader following the steps won't trip over it.

## Where reference tables belong

Tables of options are the most common thing dropped into the wrong page. They belong on a reference page, sorted the way a regular looks things up, usually by name. In a tutorial, show only the one or two options the step needs, with the values to use.

## Try it

Take your page and the answers from the worksheet. Mark each section with the kind of page it belongs to. Anything that isn't the page's main job moves to its own page with a link back.
